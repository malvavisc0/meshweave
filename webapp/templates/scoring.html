How MeshWeave scores a website

Every audit gives three scores from 0 to 100: Reachable, Answerable, and Actionable. This page lists what each score checks, how much each factor counts, and what the ratings mean.

How to read this page

The three checks follow the steps an AI agent takes on your site: first it has to reach your pages, then answer a question from them, then work out what to do next. Scores describe your website only. They do not measure or promise traffic, rankings, or mentions in AI answers.

How a score is calculated

Each check is a weighted average of its factors. Factors that could not be measured on your site are left out, and the other factors' weights are scaled up to fill the gap. The result is then slightly compressed at the top, so a score near 100 needs nearly every factor near 100.

Step What happens
Weighted average Each measured factor's score is multiplied by its weight, then divided by the total weight of the measured factors
Factor not measured Left out; the remaining weights are scaled up to add up to 100%
Top-end compression 100 × (average / 100)^1.15
Result Capped at 100, rounded to one decimal

Reachable

Can AI agents get to your pages and tell who you are? This check looks at whether AI crawlers are allowed in, whether your pages carry enough text and structured data, and whether the company is described the same way everywhere. Reviews, videos, and links to outside profiles are recorded but do not add points.

Answerable

Can AI agents answer a buyer's questions from your content? The largest factor is a fixed set of six buyer questions. A language model answers each one using only the text MeshWeave crawled from your site, and an answer counts only if it is backed by a page it can point to. The questions are the same for every site and every re-run, so scores stay comparable.

Reading the ratings

Each check has five rating bands. The lowest band means agents are blocked on that step; start there. The middle band means agents get part of the way but miss important facts. The top two bands mean the step works; remaining fixes are refinements. Every report lists its fixes in order of how many points each is expected to add.

What is rule-based and what uses a language model

Crawler access, structured data, content structure, freshness, content depth, company details, and contact paths are scored by fixed rules from the crawl. A language model is used for the six buyer questions, homepage clarity, metadata clarity, cross-page clarity, and checking contact emails. In every case it reads only your crawled pages; nothing outside your site is queried.
