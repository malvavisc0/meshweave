# Market Research: Is There Commercial Demand for AI Visibility Audits?

*Research date: 2026-09-25, fact-checked 2026-09-25. Method: web search across market reports, industry surveys, vendor pricing pages, and SEO-industry data studies. Every headline figure was traced back toward its primary source where possible — Pew, HubSpot, SE Ranking, Fractl, Adobe (via Omnibound's aggregation), and Gartner were all checked directly against the original publications. Figures are cited inline as [n] with full URLs at the bottom. The final section explains which numbers to trust and which to treat as marketing noise — several figures do come from companies selling into this category.*

---

## The short version

The pain this product addresses is real, measured, and hitting businesses **right now**: brands are visibly losing organic traffic to AI answers, awareness of the problem more than doubled in twelve months, and a paying category of audits and tracking tools already exists at roughly $250–$25,000 per engagement.

The hard truth is the opposite of "no demand": **the space is already crowded**. Dozens of SaaS trackers and agencies sell "AI visibility." What is scarce — and what MeshWeave already builds — is the *site-side diagnostic*: why AI can't read a site, what to fix first, and proof that the fix worked. That is the differentiation the data points to.

**Verdict: continue, with positioning.**

---

## 1. Demand: the pain is documented and quantified

Businesses are losing real, measurable traffic because AI now answers queries without sending clicks. Three independent lines of evidence — a panel study (Pew), a clickstream analysis (Seer Interactive), and named public casualties (HubSpot, Business Insider, global publishers) — all point the same direction.

### The headline losses

- **HubSpot, the most-cited casualty, lost roughly 70–80% of its blog's organic traffic** between November 2024 and Q2 2025, and CEO Yamini Rangan explicitly named AI Overviews on the Q1 2025 earnings call: organic search traffic is "declining globally" and "AI Overviews are giving answers, and fewer people are clicking through to websites" [1]. *Fact-check caveat:* HubSpot's own CMO Kipp Bodnar disputes the apocalyptic framing — the decline is blog traffic specifically, partly a deliberate strategy shift away from informational content toward YouTube, podcasts, and newsletters that began in 2020, and HubSpot says its transactional search volume has actually grown [2]. The honest reading: the number is real for informational content portfolios, not proof that "SEO is dead" or that every business loses this much.
- **Business Insider lost 55% of organic search traffic** between April 2022 and April 2025, followed by a 21% staff reduction [1].
- **Global publishers saw Google traffic drop roughly one-third in 2025**, per Press Gazette citing Chartbeat's network data [1]. AdExchanger's industry coverage reports publishers losing "20%, 30% and in some cases even as much as 90% of their traffic and revenue" [1].

### The causal mechanism, measured two ways

- **Pew Research (July 2025)** analyzed 68,879 real Google searches from 900 U.S. adults whose browsing was metered in March 2025. Users clicked a search-result link on only **8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% when it didn't** — nearly a doubling of the click rate without the summary, or a **46.7% relative reduction** [3]. Note the distinction: this is not "46.7% of all traffic vanished"; it is the per-visit click-rate difference on AI-summary pages. Pew also found users clicked a link *inside* the AI summary itself on just **1%** of such visits, and **ended their browsing session entirely 26% of the time** (vs. 16% without a summary) [3] — the AI answer doesn't just intercept the click, it can end the journey.
- **Seer Interactive** tracked 2.43 billion impressions across 53 brands and 5.47 million queries: organic CTR on queries where AI Overviews appeared collapsed from **1.76% to 0.61% over 14 months (a 61–65% drop depending on measurement window)**, and paid CTR on the same queries fell even harder, from 19.7% to 6.34% (−68%) [1][4]. Position-1 results lose roughly **34.5% of their CTR** the moment an AI Overview renders above them (Ahrefs, 2025) [5].
- **Zero-click search is the baseline, not the edge case.** SparkToro/Datos put US zero-click at **58.5%** (75% on mobile) [5]; Similarweb measured zero-click growing from **56% to 69%** in the year after the AI Overviews rollout [6]; Semrush measured a **93% zero-click rate inside Google's AI Mode** [5]. Different trackers, different panels, same shape: a majority of searches now end without a click.

### How widespread is the exposure?

The AI-Overview trigger rate is the number that varies most by tracker, and the spread itself is informative (all figures verified as published but methodologically different — different query samples, different geographies, different SERP-collection dates):

- **Pew (direct measurement of real user searches): 18% of searches in March 2025** produced an AI summary [3]. This is the most methodologically trustworthy figure for that date because it observes actual behavior, not scraped SERPs.
- **Conductor's 2026 benchmark: 25.11%** (across 21.9 million analyzed queries) [5].
- **Semrush (September 2025): 13% of US desktop SERPs**, but 43% zero-click when an AIO appears vs. 34% without [5].
- **Industry SERP trackers report up to 48% of queries by March 2026**, with much higher rates in high-value verticals — 88% healthcare, 83% education [7]. Treat this one as an upper bound from scraped-SERP methodology, not a settled number.

The vertical concentration matters more than the overall rate: **82% of B2B-tech queries trigger AI Overviews** [8], question-form queries trigger them 60% of the time (Pew) [3], and the queries being summarized are precisely the informational, top-of-funnel ones that historically drove organic traffic to business websites. Transactional and branded queries remain much less affected (both Pew and HubSpot confirm this pattern independently) [2][3].

### The buyer-behavior shift

- **94% of B2B buyers used generative AI during their purchase process** (6sense, 2025 Buyer Experience Report, n=4,510 B2B buyers) [5]. Methodology note: "used generative AI" spans everything from a quick ChatGPT query to deep research workflows — the direction is solid, but it doesn't mean 94% of buying decisions run through AI.
- ChatGPT alone now processes **2.5 billion queries per day** (OpenAI, July 2025) and reached roughly **900 million weekly active users by February 2026** [5]. By comparison, Google handles an estimated ~14 billion searches per day — so ChatGPT is on the order of **12–18% of Google's search volume** depending on the month and estimate [9].

### The counter-direction: AI referrals are small but exploding

Traffic isn't just disappearing — a new channel is emerging on the other side:

- **AI-referral traffic is still tiny in absolute terms: ~0.32% of all website visits globally in 2026**, up from 0.24% in 2025 and 0.02% in 2024 — a **16x increase in two years** (SE Ranking, 101,574 websites tracked, Jan 2025–Apr 2026) [10]. The earlier-cited 0.35% was the **October 2025 peak**, not the current average.
- But the quality is out of proportion to the volume. **AI-referred visitors spend 68% more time on site than organic-search visitors** (9:19 vs. 5:33 average; SE Ranking) [10]. **Adobe (1 trillion+ US retail site visits) measured holiday-2025 AI referrals converting 31% better than non-AI traffic, with revenue per visit up 254% YoY** [5].
- **US retail AI-referral traffic grew 693% YoY in the 2025 holiday season** (Adobe Digital Insights) [5], and continued at **+393% YoY in Q1 2026** [5].
- The platform mix is consolidating to five players: **ChatGPT 74.8%, Gemini 11.6%, Perplexity 7.2%, Copilot 3.5%, Claude 2.6%** of AI referral traffic — Claude grew 320% YoY (fastest by rate), Gemini 231%, while Perplexity stagnated and DeepSeek/Grok fell to near-zero [10].

Net effect: clicks are being redistributed away from informational organic search toward AI answers. The businesses on the losing side have a documented, urgent problem — and the winners of the new channel are the sites AI can actually read, extract from, and cite.

### Why this creates an audit-shaped demand

The mechanism is invisible from inside a normal SEO stack. Rankings stay flat while sessions crater — Search Console shows impressions intact, position unchanged, CTR collapsed. That is exactly the pattern a site owner cannot diagnose with existing tools, and exactly what a diagnostic crawl-plus-analysis surfaces. (See also: 73% of B2B websites saw meaningful organic traffic decline from 2024 to 2025, average YoY drop 34% [5].)

## 2. Awareness: buyers are waking up, fast

The awareness numbers support moving now, not waiting:

- **49% of marketers now actively monitor AI's impact on brand visibility, up from 22% in 2025 — more than double in twelve months** (Fractl, Q2 2026 survey of 1,008 US consumers and 150 marketers, year-over-year against the same 2025 questions) [11]. Adding those planning to monitor, **82% have LLM brand visibility on their radar; only 14% have written it off**. The shift is steepest at scale: 73% of marketers at 1,000+ employee companies actively monitor, versus 39% at companies of 10 or fewer.
- Fractl's same study quantifies the readiness gap that audits fill: **27% of brands have been inaccurately described in an AI-generated response, but only 24% have any formal monitoring process** for AI mentions [11]. More than a quarter are exposed; less than a quarter are watching.
- **54% of US marketers plan to implement GEO within 3–6 months, but only 23% currently invest in measurement** (eMarketer, Jan 2026) [5] — intent is running ahead of capability, which is the classic signal for a services-and-tooling window.
- **"generative engine optimization" gets ~4,400 monthly US searches** (July 2026, DataForSEO via Google Ads data) plus ~1,600 for the abbreviation variant — a term that barely registered two years ago [12].
- **llms.txt adoption grew from ~0.015% to 10%+ in a year**, and major platforms and SEO tools now support or discuss it [13]. *Fact-check caveat:* this figure circulates through vendor blogs (the primary claim traces to a mid-2025 site-sample scan by Pillar-based tools and was amplified by ecommerce vendors); treat it as directional evidence that the machine-readable layer is becoming a mainstream concern, not a precise adoption metric.
- The **Gartner forecast — traditional search volume dropping 25% by 2026 due to AI chatbots and virtual agents (Feb 2024)** — remains the most-cited planning anchor in the category [14]. Follow-up data (SparkToro/Datos) shows the drop proceeding unevenly, concentrated on informational queries; the direction is confirmed, the magnitude varies by vertical [5].

## 3. Competition: yes, many are already selling this

The market has split into three layers, each with established players and observable pricing.

### Layer 1 — SaaS citation/visibility trackers ($29–$1,000+/month)

These poll ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Claude, and report where a brand appears and which competitors win the answer. Entry tiers start at $27–29.

| Tool | Pricing (approx., 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Profound | $99 Starter / $399 Growth+ | Best-funded AEO platform — **~$58.5M raised (Series B, mid-2025)** [15] |
| Otterly | ~$29/mo | Budget entry point, GEO audit included [16] |
| Peec AI | €89–245/mo | Mid-market analyst positioning [16] |
| SlateHQ | $29 / $189 / $489 tiers | ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews, Gemini, Copilot [17] |
| Scrunch | $417–500/mo | Agency-tier [16] |
| LLM Pulse | $69/mo+ | API-first [18] |
| Conductor | Enterprise (custom) | Enterprise-grade; publishes the 21.9M-query benchmark cited above [15] |

Benchmarks: the **average AI-visibility tracker costs ~$337/month** [19]; **agencies running 5–50 clients typically pay $200–750/month** for tracking tooling [20]. Total cost of ownership is higher in practice: monitoring-only tools at $25–50/month routinely still need **$5,000–15,000 in consulting** to turn citation data into strategy [21] — the consulting-translation gap that MeshWeave's fix-list directly attacks. Disclosed AEO-tool funding collectively moved past **$200M between 2023 and 2025/26** [9].

### Layer 2 — Agency audit and remediation services ($250–$25,000)

Audits are already a priced, billable deliverable:

- Standalone **AI visibility audits: $250–$1,500**; agencies frequently run them free as a lead magnet that closes **$2,000–$10,000** implementation or retainer work [22].
- Standard large-agency audits: **$1,500–$5,000**; the broader published range is **$700–$25,000** [23].
- "Enterprise" one-time audits: **$8,000–$25,000** [24].
- Established GEO/AEO agency rankings already exist (GenOptima, First Page Sage, Single Grain's "12 best," Adogy's world guide) — a service category mature enough to have leaderboards [25][26][27][28].
- GEO retainers specifically: entry-level in the low thousands per month, mid-market in the mid four figures, enterprise full-service in the low five figures [1].

### Layer 3 — Direct analogues (the closest structural competitors)

Two tools do something structurally close to MeshWeave — automated crawl-side scoring rather than external visibility polling:

- **Rank Prompt — "AI SEO Audit"**: audits robots.txt, llms.txt, and structured data for AI crawler access, plus schema problems and core-web-vitals blockers, with scoring and a "run free audit" CTA [29].
- **Apify — "AI Readiness Checker" actor**: crawls a site for AI crawler access, llms.txt, structured data, answer-ready content, and trust signals, and returns **scores and prioritized fixes** [30].

Both validate the crawl-side diagnostic concept. Neither closes the loop into a full fix-list → re-run → measured-diff cycle, and neither is built for white-label agency delivery. That gap is where MeshWeave sits.

## 4. The differentiation gap (where MeshWeave wins)

Nearly the entire product category is **external visibility tracking** — it answers "where does my brand appear in AI answers today?" Almost none of it answers the more useful question: **"why can't AI agents read my site correctly — and exactly what do I fix first?"**

The citation research explains why the second question is the commercially important one:

- **59.6% of AI Overview citations come from URLs not ranking in the organic top 20** (AirOps/Kevin Indig, 2026 State of AI Search) — ranking no longer guarantees being cited, so "check your rankings" cannot tell you why AI skips you [5].
- **85% of brand mentions in AI answers originate on third-party pages**, not the brand's own site [5] — but the 15% you control is still the necessary precondition, and it's the only part you can fix directly.
- **Pages with sequential headings and rich schema get cited 2.8x more; pages not updated in 3+ months are 3x more likely to lose citations** [5] — i.e., the fix list is technical, concrete, and re-checkable.
- **Only 30% of brands stay visible from one AI answer to the next** [5] — visibility is volatile, which makes repeatable diagnosis (re-run and diff) valuable rather than one-off.

MeshWeave's three-check model maps directly onto the buyer's three anxieties. The anxieties are market context (verbatim buyer language); the answer is always site-side — the website is the controllable prerequisite:

| Anxiety | MeshWeave check | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| "AI skips us — a competitor answers instead" | **Answerable** | Can an agent pull a supported, direct answer from our content? |
| "AI never recommends us" | **Reachable + Answerable** | Does the site give agents consistent context and supported answers to work from? |
| "Buyers who arrive through AI give up" | **Actionable** | Can an agent understand the offer and find a credible next step? |

The differentiators the verified data supports:

- **The fix list is prioritized by computed impact** (`expected_points`, re-checkable against observed deltas on the next run) — tracking tools stop at reporting absence from answers; MeshWeave ends at "fix these three things first." The Siftly data point ($5K–15K consulting to translate raw citation data [21]) is the exact labor cost MeshWeave automates away.
- **The diff is proof of work.** Predicted-vs-observed per-fix delta on re-run is precisely the artifact that turns a $0 audit into a $250–$1,500 white-labeled deliverable and a $2,000–$10,000 retainer [22]. It also matches what agencies say retains accounts in this cycle: owning the diagnosis with attribution percentages, not defending traffic charts [1].
- **White-label is built in** — unbranded `report.md`, no MeshWeave footer — which the tracking SaaS doesn't do (they sell their own brand's dashboard). White-label delivery is a proven agency model elsewhere in marketing (Demand Local built an entire agency channel on it) [1].
- **MeshWeave addresses only the controllable prerequisite: the website.** External answer tracking is a separate category with its own tools; MeshWeave neither tracks what answer engines say nor positions itself as an alternative to tools that do. It diagnoses and fixes the one surface the client fully controls — the surface every answer is grounded in.

## 5. Recommendation

**Continue, with clear positioning** — the evidence supports the product, but not the market's generic visibility pitch. Present MeshWeave as the controllable prerequisite: the website is the only surface the client fully controls, and every answer is grounded in it. MeshWeave is a site-side diagnostic pill — never a visibility tracker or a tracker alternative.

1. **Position as the audit engine for agencies**, not a tracking SaaS. Agencies are proven buyers with existing budgets ($200–750/mo tooling, $250–1,500 per audit deliverable, and 54% of US marketers planning GEO work within 3–6 months [5]), and they are the channel through which client budgets funnel.
2. **Lead with the fix-list + diff loop**, not the scores. Scoring is being commoditized by funded SaaS at $29/mo; the *actionable, provable remediation roadmap* is where the money is ($2K–10K retainers [22]).
3. **Ship the API-first story harder.** The funded players [15] and developer-adjacent actors [30] both succeed via pipeline-friendly interfaces, and MeshWeave's v1 bulk API already matches that shape.
4. **Do not try to out-track the trackers.** External answer polling is a different, capital-intensive game with $200M+ of funded competition [9]. MeshWeave's crawl-side diagnosis is cheaper to run and structurally different.
5. **Time the awareness window deliberately.** Monitoring adoption doubled in a year (22%→49%) but measurement maturity is lagging badly (only 23% invest in measurement [5]). The buyers know they have the problem; they don't yet have a standard way to solve it. First movers into that gap get to define the deliverable.

## 6. Caveats on the data

Honesty required before these numbers go into a pitch:

- **Market-size figures conflict wildly.** AEO tool estimates range from **$160.9M** (CAGR 43.4%, Dimension Market Research) [31] to **$573M in 2025 → $816M** (QY Research) [32] to "$8.3B+" in poised-for-growth framing (HiGoodie) [9]; GEO-platform estimates land at **$365.4M for 2026** (Coherent Market Insights) [33] or **$848M (2025) → $33.7B (2034)** (Dimension Market Research, cited by Superlines) [5]. Treat every absolute number as directional. **The growth rates (~43–50% CAGR, roughly consistent across trackers) and the behavior studies (Pew, Seer, Adobe, SE Ranking) are the trustworthy signal, not the TAM estimates.**
- **The HubSpot number is the most quoted and most oversimplified figure in the category.** It is blog traffic, it is partly a deliberate content-strategy shift HubSpot started in 2020, and HubSpot itself disputes the "80% loss, SEO is dead" framing [2]. Use it as "informational-content portfolios are structurally exposed," never as "every business loses 80%."
- **AI-Overview trigger rates conflict across sources by a factor of ~3.7** (18% Pew [3] to 48% SERP-trackers [7]). Methodology differs: real-user panels versus scraped SERPs, different months, different query mixes. When citing a rate, name the source and date; expect the audience's own numbers to differ.
- **Pew's 46.7% is a relative per-visit click-rate reduction (8% vs 15%), not a share of total traffic lost.** Conflating the two overstates the damage and hands a correction to any informed listener.
- **Many sources are SEO-vendor marketing blogs** (Omnibound, HiGoodie, Indexly, Siftly, IT Marketz...) that sell into this category, and several quote each other. This fact-check deliberately favored primary or reputable secondary sources: Pew [3], HubSpot's own explainer [2], SE Ranking's published methodology [10], Fractl's named survey [11], Gartner [14], Adobe Digital Insights (1 trillion+ visits) and 6sense (n=4,510) via Omnibound's aggregated citations [5], Ahrefs [5], Similarweb [6].
- **Tool pricing changes fast** in this space; treat all price points as order-of-magnitude, verified April–September 2026.
- **None of this measures MeshWeave's own funnel** (usage, inquiry, API adoption) — that data lives in the `funnel_events` / `api_key_usage_daily` tables and is the real go/no-go evidence once enough of it accumulates. External market data justifies continued building; only internal data justifies scaling spend.
